01The problem: scale vs. personalisation

India's competitive-exam coaching market has a structural trade-off: high-touch coaching can offer individualised support but doesn't scale cheaply; affordable, mass-market platforms reach far more students but historically struggle to personalise at that volume.

Physics Wallah built its position on affordability and reach. The open question this case study explores is whether AI can let a mass-market platform deliver some of the benefits of high-touch coaching, knowing what a student is weak at, what to study next, and when they need help, without a proportional increase in human cost.

Problem statement

A student on a mass-market platform has access to hundreds of hours of content and thousands of questions, but often lacks personalised decision support: what to study today, what they're actually weak at, and whether they're on track.

Framing rule

This case study does not assume PW is cheaper, larger, or has better or worse AI than Allen, Unacademy or Vedantu. Those are open research questions, not established facts.

03Primary user: the at-risk JEE/NEET dropper

This persona is prioritised because droppers face the highest stakes with the least slack: they've already attempted the exam once, have known and unknown gaps, and are highly sensitive to falling behind schedule.

"I have hundreds of hours of content and thousands of questions, but I don't know what I should study today, what I'm actually weak at, or whether I'm on track for my target score."

The problem is too much content with too little personalisation: not too little content. That distinction changes what you build.

Key current-state friction: generic schedules, delayed doubt resolution, difficulty self-diagnosing root weaknesses, and poor prioritisation, all of which compound as a student falls further behind.

05The solution: AI Learning Coach

Positioned as a 24/7 personalised academic operating system, and explicitly not a replacement for teachers. It answers five questions for every student: What should I study? What am I weak at? What should I practise? Am I on track? When do I need human help?

FeatureWhat it does
Diagnostic + Learning ProfileTopic-level mastery map from a diagnostic test plus performance history
AI Study PlanA specific daily plan (topic, duration, question count) that adapts to progress and fatigue
Multimodal Doubt SolverStudent photographs a question; AI extracts, classifies and explains it
Exam Readiness ScorePredicts topic-level risk and overall readiness, not just a raw percentage
Slippage DetectionFlags when a student is falling behind syllabus pace, before it compounds
Intervention EngineRecommends a concrete recovery sequence, escalating to a teacher if needed
Teacher CopilotSurfaces at-risk students with an AI diagnosis so teachers focus where it matters

06Human-in-the-loop design

The AI should not answer every doubt blindly. Confidence-tiered routing keeps humans in the loop for exactly the cases where AI is least reliable:

ConfidenceAction
HighAI answers directly
MediumAI answers, with a lightweight verification option
LowRouted to a human teacher
On thresholds

Any specific confidence cutoff (e.g. 95%) is illustrative only. Real thresholds require calibration against an expert-labelled evaluation set before launch, a number picked in a doc is a guess, not a threshold.

The teacher's role in the future state

AI absorbs first-line doubts, repetition and progress tracking, freeing teachers for complex problems, motivation, strategy and high-risk students, increasing teacher leverage rather than replacing them.

07AI architecture

Student events→ Data platform→ Feature store→ Recommendation engine→ AI orchestrator→ Student + teacher experience↺ Model evaluation

Why a knowledge graph + RAG, not a freeform LLM

Subject→ Chapter→ Concept→ Sub-concept→ Question→ Common error

A knowledge graph lets the system locate exactly which sub-concept a student is weak in, Physics → Mechanics → Rotational Motion → Moment of Inertia, rather than reporting "you're weak at Physics." The doubt solver retrieves from approved course material, notes and question banks (RAG) rather than letting the LLM invent explanations, which matters when a wrong formula has real academic consequences.

LayerUsed for
Traditional ML / rulesCompletion tracking, score calculation, slippage detection, difficulty calibration
LLM (RAG-grounded)Explaining free-form doubts, personalised explanations, summarising learning history

08Risks & guardrails

  • Hallucination: a wrong formula or explanation; mitigated by RAG grounding and confidence-based escalation.
  • Dependency: students may stop reasoning independently if answers are handed over too readily. The solver should default to explaining concepts, not just final answers.
  • Misdiagnosis: the system may mislabel a weakness; teacher review and student-reported corrections act as a check.
  • Bias: different student groups could receive uneven support quality; requires evaluation segmented by cohort, not just in aggregate.
  • Privacy: performance and doubt data are sensitive and require strict access controls and audit logging.

09Product requirements

Goals
  • Give every student a clear, personalised answer to "what should I study next?"
  • Detect falling-behind students early enough to intervene before it compounds
  • Increase teacher leverage rather than replace teacher judgement
Non-goals (v1)
  • A fully autonomous AI tutor with no human escalation path
  • Promising a specific exam score or outcome
  • Replacing structured live or recorded teaching content

Representative user stories

  • As a dropper, I want to know exactly which sub-topics I'm weakest in, not just an overall percentage.
  • As a student, I want a specific daily plan, not just a chapter-level schedule.
  • As a student, I want to photograph a doubt and get an explained solution, not just an answer.
  • As a student falling behind, I want to be alerted before a test reveals it.
  • As a teacher, I want an AI-prioritised list of at-risk students with a suggested diagnosis.
Accuracy
  • Doubt-solver answers validated against an expert-labelled benchmark before launch
Latency
  • Study-plan and doubt-solver responses in seconds, not minutes
Privacy
  • Student performance and doubt history access-controlled and audit-logged
Accessibility
  • Works on low-bandwidth connections and lower-end devices, non-negotiable for a mass-market base

11Metrics

15Summary

One line

Designed an AI Learning Coach strategy that lets a mass-market exam-prep platform deliver personalised study plans, doubt resolution and early intervention at a fraction of human-only tutoring cost.

The 50-word version

Affordable exam-prep platforms reach millions but struggle to personalise at scale. I designed an AI Learning Coach, diagnostic profiling, adaptive study plans, a RAG-grounded multimodal doubt solver, and slippage detection with teacher escalation, to close that gap while keeping teachers focused on where human judgement matters most.

What this demonstrates

  • Designed an AI product strategy giving a mass-market EdTech platform student-level personalisation (diagnostics, adaptive plans, slippage detection) at a fraction of human-tutoring marginal cost.
  • Proposed a RAG-grounded, confidence-tiered architecture separating ML (tracking and prediction), LLM (explanation and tutoring) and human escalation by task suitability.
  • Developed a learning-outcome-first metrics framework and experiment plan (North Star plus four A/B tests) to validate AI-driven retention impact before scaling.
