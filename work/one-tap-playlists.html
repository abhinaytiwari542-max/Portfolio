One-Tap Playlists

I kept discovering songs I loved through autoplay, but the moment the session ended, I had no easy way to turn that discovery into a playlist.

01The problem

Music streaming platforms are very good at discovery: a user plays a song, and autoplay or radio surfaces a stream of similar tracks. Often several of those recommended songs genuinely land. What the product doesn't handle well is the moment right after: turning a good listening session into something the user keeps.

Today, converting a liked sequence of recommended songs into a playlist requires remembering each song, finding it again, opening it, and adding it individually, repeated per song. Most users abandon the process partway through, or never start it.

Framing

This is not a claim that recommendation algorithms are bad. Discovery is working. The gap is between discovery and collection: a persistent-artifact problem, not a relevance problem. That distinction is what keeps this out of the recommender team's backlog and inside a feature you can actually ship.

Opportunity: convert passive music discovery into an actionable playlist with minimal user effort.

03The solution: Discovery Session

Every recommendation or autoplay stream is automatically tracked as a temporary Discovery Session: songs played, skipped, replayed, liked, and listening duration. At any point the user can save it as a playlist with one tap.

Save all
  • Every song in the session
Save favourites
  • Only songs the user liked or replayed
Customise
  • Manually select individual tracks
AI curate
  • Describe an intent ("make this more chill," "good for driving") and let AI select and order

04Where AI adds value

The recommendation engine (collaborative filtering and embeddings-based similarity) stays responsible for candidate generation and ranking, because that's what it's good at. An AI orchestration layer sits on top and handles the parts a traditional ranking system can't:

  • Interpreting natural-language intent ("make this a 45-minute late-night playlist")
  • Reordering and trimming a session into a coherent, mood-consistent playlist
  • Explaining why songs were included, to build trust in an auto-generated list
  • Supporting conversational edits after the first draft ("swap out anything too high-energy")
Key architectural decision

The LLM never generates songs freeform. It selects and orders only from a candidate set already produced by the recommendation system and the user's own session history. This eliminates hallucinated tracks by construction rather than by prompting, and keeps the recommender, not the LLM, as the source of truth for what music exists and is licensed.

05Product requirements

Goals
  • Let users convert a recommendation session into a playlist in one tap
  • Increase the share of discovery sessions that result in a saved playlist
  • Preserve full user control over the final playlist
Non-goals (v1)
  • Fully autonomous playlist management without user review
  • Cross-platform import from other streaming services
  • Social or collaborative session sharing

Representative user stories

  • As a listener, I want to save my recent discovery session so I don't lose songs I enjoyed.
  • As a listener, I want to save only the songs I actively liked, not the whole session.
  • As a listener, I want to describe a vibe ("driving," "chill") and get a playlist matching it.
  • As a listener, I want to preview and remove songs before the playlist is saved.
  • As a listener, I want duplicate songs and artists automatically minimised.
Latency
  • Session-to-preview generation under roughly 3 seconds for AI curation
Reliability
  • Session data must survive app restarts and network drops
Privacy
  • Session and listening data used for curation only, never shared externally
Accessibility
  • Full save and curate flow usable via screen reader and keyboard

06MVP & prioritisation

MVP scope

  • Automatic session tracking during recommendation and autoplay
  • Save All and Select Songs flows
  • Duplicate removal
  • Basic AI ordering, sequencing only, not full conversational curation

Conversational AI curation, social sharing and voice creation are deferred. They add cost and complexity without being required to prove the core hypothesis that one-tap saving increases conversion.

09Trade-offs & risks

  • Automation vs. control: AI curation must stay editable; users should never lose the ability to override it.
  • Personalisation vs. discovery: over-personalising saved playlists risks narrowing future recommendations into an echo chamber.
  • AI quality vs. latency and cost: richer curation (LLM reasoning over audio features) costs more per playlist and adds latency; the MVP intentionally uses lightweight ordering.
  • Session scope: how much of a long session to offer for saving (10 songs vs. the entire session) affects both usefulness and clutter.
  • Groundedness risk: AI curation must only ever select from real catalogue and session tracks. This is enforced architecturally, not just by prompting.

10Roadmap

Phase 1 · MVP

Session tracking, Save All / Select, duplicate removal, basic ordering.

Phase 2

AI curation from natural-language intent ("make this chill").

Phase 3

Conversational playlist editing.

Phase 4

Proactive AI music companion, for example "build me a 60-minute playlist for tomorrow's drive," informed by history, mood and context.

11Business impact

Where the value would show up, to be quantified post-launch:

  • Higher playlist creation and songs saved per active user
  • Increased repeat listening and session length via revisited playlists
  • Higher switching cost as personalised libraries grow
  • Indirect contribution to retention and premium conversion through stronger perceived personalisation

12Summary

One line

The 50-word version

I identified a discovery-to-collection gap in music streaming: users enjoy recommended songs but rarely convert them into playlists. I designed Discovery Session, a feature that tracks recommendation sessions and lets users save or AI-curate them in one tap, backed by a full PRD, metrics tree and experiment plan.

What this demonstrates

  • Identified and scoped a discovery-to-collection product gap in music streaming; defined JTBD, personas and a research plan to validate the opportunity.
  • Designed an AI orchestration layer constrained to a controlled candidate set, eliminating hallucinated recommendations by architecture rather than prompting.
  • Built a full experimentation and metrics framework, with a North Star, supporting and guardrail metrics, and three A/B test designs, to measure discovery-to-playlist conversion.
