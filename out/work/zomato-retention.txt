14:T5279,<section id="problem">
      <h2><span class="num">01</span>The problem</h2>
      <p>Zomato competes in a multi-homing marketplace: most customers have both Zomato and Swiggy installed, so switching cost is near zero and every order is a fresh competitive decision. The instinctive lever is discounting, but discounts are easy to match, expensive to sustain, and can train customers to only order when a promotion is active.</p>
      <p>The real question is not how to give bigger discounts. It's how to retain customers <strong>efficiently</strong>: without discount spend growing faster than retention does.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Problem statement</span>
        <p>Zomato needs to retain price-sensitive customers in a multi-homing marketplace without continuously increasing discount spend.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="callout callout--hyp">
        <span class="callout__label">Working hypothesis</span>
        <p>Giving the right customer the right incentive at the right time may retain more customers per rupee spent than giving everyone the same discount.</p>
      </div>

      <h3>Customer vs. business tension</h3>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Customer wants</th><th>Business wants</th><th>Tension</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody><tr>
          <td>Good prices, reliable delivery, fast issue resolution</td>
          <td>Higher repeat orders, lower churn, lower promo cost</td>
          <td>More discount may lift short-term orders but erode long-term margin</td>
        </tr></tbody>
      </table></div>
    </section>

    <section id="segments">
      <h2><span class="num">02</span>Customer segments</h2>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Segment</th><th>Profile</th><th>Retention objective</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Heavy Users</td><td>8-15 orders/month</td><td>Protect retention with low-cost loyalty benefits</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Discount Seekers</td><td>Orders mainly when offers exist</td><td>Lift frequency without escalating discount dependency</td></tr>
          <tr><td>At-Risk Users</td><td>Previously active, frequency declining</td><td>Prevent churn before it happens</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Churned Users</td><td>No order in a long period</td><td>Win back at acceptable cost</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Premium Users</td><td>High order value and frequency</td><td>Grow lifetime value, not just order count</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="callout callout--hyp">
        <span class="callout__label">Research plan, not yet conducted</span>
        <p>15 customer interviews covering platform choice, discount sensitivity, delivery reliability and support experience, plus hypotheses to be tested, e.g. <em>"customers with declining order frequency are more likely to churn,"</em> and <em>"delivery reliability matters more than discount size for some segments."</em></p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="market">
      <h2><span class="num">03</span>Marketplace dynamics &amp; competitive landscape</h2>
      <p>Zomato and Swiggy both operate two-sided marketplaces (restaurants + customers), and neither platform has an inherent lock-in. No claim is made here that either has better discounts, service or restaurant selection, those are open questions for research, and likely vary by city and segment.</p>

      <h3>The multi-homing flywheel</h3>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">More restaurants</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Better selection</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">More customers</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">More orders</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">More restaurant demand</span><span class="flow__arw">↺</span>
      </div>
      <p>Discounts can accelerate this flywheel in the short term, more orders means more restaurant interest. But if customers only respond to price, the flywheel becomes dependent on ever-increasing subsidy rather than genuine product loyalty.</p>

      <h3>Dimensions to research (not assumed)</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Acquisition approach and promotional intensity</li>
        <li>Loyalty / membership programme design</li>
        <li>Delivery reliability and speed</li>
        <li>Customer support responsiveness and issue resolution</li>
        <li>Personalisation of offers and recommendations</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

    <section id="funnel">
      <h2><span class="num">04</span>Retention funnel &amp; the key insight</h2>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">New user</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">First order</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Second order</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Repeat</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Habitual</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Loyal</span>
      </div>
      <p>The <strong>first → second order</strong> transition is typically the highest-leverage drop-off point. It's the first evidence of habit formation, and the point where a generic new-user discount stops being the reason to return.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Key insight</span>
        <p>The goal isn't to maximise discounts. It's to maximise <strong>incremental retention per rupee of incentive spend</strong>. A customer who would have reordered anyway represents wasted discount spend; a customer who reorders <em>only</em> because of the incentive represents real ROI.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="formula">Incremental Retention ROI <span>=</span> Incremental retained customers <span>/</span> Incremental discount spend<br>
Cost per Retained Customer <span>=</span> Total incremental incentive cost <span>/</span> Incrementally retained customers</div>
    </section>

    <section id="solution">
      <h2><span class="num">05</span>The solution: Zomato Smart Rewards</h2>
      <p>An AI-powered retention engine that decides <strong>who</strong> should receive an incentive, <strong>when</strong>, <strong>what kind</strong>, <strong>how much</strong> to spend, and <strong>why</strong>: rather than applying one discount rule to everyone.</p>

      <h3>Decision flow</h3>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">Customer data</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Feature engineering</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Churn model</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Offer propensity</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Expected incremental order</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Budget-constrained optimiser</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Best incentive</span>
      </div>

      <h3>Which AI technique for which job</h3>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Layer</th><th>Used for</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Traditional ML</td><td>Churn prediction, segmentation, incentive-response prediction</td><td>Structured, high-volume, well-suited to classification/regression</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Optimisation</td><td>Incentive selection, budget allocation, experiment allocation</td><td>Constrained decision-making under a fixed budget</td></tr>
          <tr><td>LLM</td><td>Support-ticket summarisation, complaint classification, personalised offer copy</td><td>Best for language understanding and generation, not numeric prediction</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="callout callout--decision">
        <span class="callout__label">Architecture decision</span>
        <p>An LLM is <strong>not</strong> used to predict churn or set discount amounts. That's a job for calibrated ML models where precision and auditability matter, and where you need to explain to a finance team why a specific rupee amount was chosen.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="uplift">
      <h2><span class="num">06</span>Uplift modelling, the central idea</h2>
      <p>A high churn-probability score alone doesn't justify an incentive: some high-risk customers would churn regardless, and some low-risk customers wouldn't respond to an offer either. What matters is the <strong>incremental effect of the intervention</strong>.</p>

      <div class="formula">Uplift <span>=</span> P(retention | incentive) <span>−</span> P(retention | no incentive)</div>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Customer</th><th>Retention w/o incentive</th><th>Retention w/ ₹100</th><th>Uplift</th><th>Good target?</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>A</td><td>40%</td><td>60%</td><td>+20 pts</td><td><span class="pri pri--mvp">Yes, incentive changes behaviour</span></td></tr>
          <tr><td>B</td><td>85%</td><td>87%</td><td>+2 pts</td><td><span class="pri pri--later">No, would return anyway</span></td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Why this matters</span>
        <p>Customer A is the better incentive target even though Customer B has a far higher absolute retention probability. That gap is the difference between <strong>propensity modelling</strong> and <strong>uplift modelling</strong>: and it's the mechanism that keeps incentive spend efficient rather than merely well-targeted.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="recovery">
      <h2><span class="num">07</span>Service recovery as a retention lever</h2>
      <p>Customer service is treated as part of the retention system, not a separate function. When a customer experiences a late delivery, missing item or quality issue, the system recommends an appropriate recovery action, refund, credit, free delivery, or priority support, with human policy as the final authority for sensitive or high-value cases.</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Situation</th><th>Generic approach</th><th>Personalised approach</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>At-risk, price-insensitive customer</td><td>"₹100 OFF" banner</td><td>"Your next delivery is on us", matches known delivery-fee sensitivity</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Churned 30+ days</td><td>No message</td><td>"We've missed you, ₹100 off your next order"</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Loyal, frequent customer</td><td>Another discount</td><td>"You've ordered 10 times this month, free delivery this weekend"</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Recent late delivery</td><td>Nothing / generic apology</td><td>"Sorry your last order was late, free delivery next time"</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>
    </section>

    <section id="prd">
      <h2><span class="num">08</span>Product requirements</h2>
      <div class="split">
        <div>
          <h6>Goals</h6>
          <ul>
            <li>Increase incremental retention per rupee of incentive spend</li>
            <li>Replace blanket discounting with targeted, explainable interventions</li>
            <li>Treat service recovery as a first-class retention lever</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <div>
          <h6>Non-goals (v1)</h6>
          <ul>
            <li>Fully autonomous incentive agent with no human oversight</li>
            <li>Dynamic real-time pricing</li>
            <li>Restaurant-side supply optimisation</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3>Representative user stories</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>As an <strong>at-risk customer</strong>, I want an offer relevant to why I might leave, not a generic discount.</li>
        <li>As a <strong>loyal customer</strong>, I don't want to be given increasingly large discounts I don't need.</li>
        <li>As a <strong>customer who had a bad delivery</strong>, I want proactive recovery, not silence.</li>
        <li>As a <strong>product team</strong>, we want to measure incremental impact, not just coupon redemption.</li>
        <li>As a <strong>product team</strong>, we want every AI-recommended incentive to have a clear, auditable reason.</li>
      </ul>

      <h3>Key non-functional requirements</h3>
      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Latency</h6><ul><li>Offer decision available before app-open render, &lt;500ms from cache/precompute</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Explainability</h6><ul><li>Every recommended incentive carries a human-readable reason code</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Privacy</h6><ul><li>Order history, location and support data used only for retention modelling, never shared externally</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Scalability</h6><ul><li>Churn and propensity scoring runs across the full active user base daily</li></ul></div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="mvp">

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Why a holdout group matters</span>
        <p>Some customers would reorder with no incentive at all. Without a <strong>permanent</strong> control/holdout group, Zomato risks attributing organic reorders to discounts and systematically overstating incentive ROI, the failure mode that makes discount budgets grow forever while looking successful in every dashboard.</p>

    <section id="ethics">
      <h2><span class="num">12</span>Ethics, fairness &amp; trade-offs</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Price discrimination</strong>: different customers seeing different offers must be explainable and non-exploitative, not arbitrary.</li>
        <li><strong>Vulnerable customers</strong>: avoid incentive designs that exploit compulsive ordering behaviour.</li>
        <li><strong>Transparency</strong>: personalisation should be understandable to the customer, not a black box.</li>
        <li><strong>Privacy</strong>: order history, location and support interactions require strict access controls.</li>
        <li><strong>Notification fatigue</strong>: more targeting doesn't mean more messages. Over-targeting is a guardrail metric, not a success metric.</li>
        <li><strong>Model risk</strong>: a wrong churn or uplift prediction should default to a low-cost, low-risk action, not an aggressive discount.</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

      <h2><span class="num">14</span>Business impact &amp; unit economics</h2>
      <div class="formula">Contribution Value <span>=</span> CLV <span>−</span> Acquisition Cost <span>−</span> Discount Cost <span>−</span> Delivery Subsidy <span>−</span> Support Cost</div>
      <p>The framework's goal is to move discount spend from a blanket cost line to a targeted investment: reducing spend on customers who'd return anyway, and concentrating it on customers whose behaviour a well-chosen incentive can actually change. No revenue or margin figures are asserted here. They would be established through the experiments above.</p>

      <h2><span class="num">15</span>Summary</h2>
      <div class="summary">
        <h3>One line</h3>
        <p>Designed an AI-powered retention engine for Zomato that replaces blanket discounting with uplift-modelled, budget-constrained incentives and proactive service recovery.</p>

        <h3>The 50-word version</h3>
        <p>In a multi-homing food-delivery market, discounts alone don't build loyalty. I designed an AI retention system that predicts churn, models the incremental effect (uplift) of an incentive per customer, and selects the lowest-cost intervention (including service recovery) likely to actually change behaviour, validated through holdout-controlled experiments.</p>

        <h3>What this demonstrates</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>Reframed a discounting problem as an incremental-retention-ROI problem, then designed an uplift-modelling approach to target incentives at customers whose behaviour would actually change.</li>
          <li>Designed an AI system architecture separating ML (prediction), optimisation (budget-constrained decisioning) and LLM (language tasks) <em>by capability</em>, rather than defaulting to one technique.</li>
          <li>Built a full experimentation framework, including a permanent holdout group, to isolate incremental incentive impact from organic reorder behaviour.</li>
        </ul>
