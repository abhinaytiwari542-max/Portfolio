17:T3bd4,<section id="problem">
      <h2><span class="num">01</span>The problem</h2>
      <p>Music streaming platforms are very good at discovery: a user plays a song, and autoplay or radio surfaces a stream of similar tracks. Often several of those recommended songs genuinely land. What the product doesn't handle well is the moment right after: turning a good listening session into <strong>something the user keeps</strong>.</p>
      <p>Today, converting a liked sequence of recommended songs into a playlist requires remembering each song, finding it again, opening it, and adding it individually, repeated per song. Most users abandon the process partway through, or never start it.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--decision">
        <span class="callout__label">Framing</span>
        <p>This is not a claim that recommendation algorithms are bad. <strong>Discovery is working.</strong> The gap is between discovery and collection: a persistent-artifact problem, not a relevance problem. That distinction is what keeps this out of the recommender team's backlog and inside a feature you can actually ship.</p>
      </div>

      <p><strong>Opportunity:</strong> convert passive music discovery into an actionable playlist with minimal user effort.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="jtbd">
      <h2><span class="num">02</span>Users &amp; job to be done</h2>
      <div class="pquote">When I discover several songs I enjoy during a recommendation session, I want to save them together, so I can revisit them later without re-finding each one.</div>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Persona</th><th>Behaviour</th><th>Primary pain</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Discovery Listener (18 to 25), primary target</td><td>Leans on autoplay and radio; likes new artists; doesn't maintain playlists</td><td>Finds great songs, then forgets them entirely</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Playlist Curator (20 to 30)</td><td>Builds mood and activity playlists deliberately</td><td>Manual curation is slow and repetitive</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Passive Listener (25 to 40)</td><td>Relies on autoplay, rarely opens playlist tools</td><td>Wants good music without managing it</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <p>The <strong>Discovery Listener</strong> is the primary target: this group generates the most recommendation-session volume and has the largest gap between "songs enjoyed" and "songs saved," which is where the conversion opportunity is biggest.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--hyp">
        <span class="callout__label">Research plan, not yet conducted</span>
        <p>10 user interviews (for example, "What do you do when you hear a song you really like?" and "Have you ever wanted to save multiple recommended songs at once?") plus an 8 to 10 question survey on discovery and playlist habits. Anything referenced below is a hypothesis to validate before build.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="solution">
      <h2><span class="num">03</span>The solution: Discovery Session</h2>
      <p>Every recommendation or autoplay stream is automatically tracked as a temporary <strong>Discovery Session</strong>: songs played, skipped, replayed, liked, and listening duration. At any point the user can save it as a playlist with one tap.</p>

      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Save all</h6><ul><li>Every song in the session</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Save favourites</h6><ul><li>Only songs the user liked or replayed</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Customise</h6><ul><li>Manually select individual tracks</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>AI curate</h6><ul><li>Describe an intent ("make this more chill," "good for driving") and let AI select and order</li></ul></div>
      </div>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Step</th><th>Current experience</th><th>With Discovery Session</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>1</td><td>User enjoys several recommended songs in a row</td><td>Same, no change to discovery</td></tr>
          <tr><td>2</td><td>User tries to remember which songs they liked</td><td>Session is already tracked automatically</td></tr>
          <tr><td>3</td><td>User searches for and re-opens each song</td><td>User taps "Save Session" once</td></tr>
          <tr><td>4</td><td>User adds each song to a playlist one at a time</td><td>User reviews an auto-generated preview, removes anything unwanted</td></tr>
          <tr><td>5</td><td>User gives up partway or saves only a few songs</td><td>Playlist is created in one step, named and ready to revisit</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>
    </section>

    <section id="ai">
      <h2><span class="num">04</span>Where AI adds value</h2>
      <p>The recommendation engine (collaborative filtering and embeddings-based similarity) stays responsible for candidate generation and ranking, because that's what it's good at. An AI orchestration layer sits on top and handles the parts a traditional ranking system can't:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Interpreting natural-language intent ("make this a 45-minute late-night playlist")</li>
        <li>Reordering and trimming a session into a coherent, mood-consistent playlist</li>
        <li>Explaining <em>why</em> songs were included, to build trust in an auto-generated list</li>
        <li>Supporting conversational edits after the first draft ("swap out anything too high-energy")</li>
      </ul>

      <div class="callout callout--decision">
        <span class="callout__label">Key architectural decision</span>
        <p>The LLM <strong>never generates songs freeform.</strong> It selects and orders only from a candidate set already produced by the recommendation system and the user's own session history. This eliminates hallucinated tracks <em>by construction</em> rather than by prompting, and keeps the recommender, not the LLM, as the source of truth for what music exists and is licensed.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="formula">{ <span>"playlist_name"</span>: "", <span>"songs"</span>: [track_ids], <span>"reason"</span>: "", <span>"estimated_duration"</span>: "" }</div>
    </section>

    <section id="prd">
      <h2><span class="num">05</span>Product requirements</h2>
      <div class="split">
        <div>
          <h6>Goals</h6>
          <ul>
            <li>Let users convert a recommendation session into a playlist in one tap</li>
            <li>Increase the share of discovery sessions that result in a saved playlist</li>
            <li>Preserve full user control over the final playlist</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <div>
          <h6>Non-goals (v1)</h6>
          <ul>
            <li>Fully autonomous playlist management without user review</li>
            <li>Cross-platform import from other streaming services</li>
            <li>Social or collaborative session sharing</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3>Representative user stories</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>As a listener, I want to save my recent discovery session so I don't lose songs I enjoyed.</li>
        <li>As a listener, I want to save only the songs I actively liked, not the whole session.</li>
        <li>As a listener, I want to describe a vibe ("driving," "chill") and get a playlist matching it.</li>
        <li>As a listener, I want to preview and remove songs before the playlist is saved.</li>
        <li>As a listener, I want duplicate songs and artists automatically minimised.</li>
      </ul>

      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Latency</h6><ul><li>Session-to-preview generation under roughly 3 seconds for AI curation</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Reliability</h6><ul><li>Session data must survive app restarts and network drops</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Privacy</h6><ul><li>Session and listening data used for curation only, never shared externally</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Accessibility</h6><ul><li>Full save and curate flow usable via screen reader and keyboard</li></ul></div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="mvp">
      <h2><span class="num">06</span>MVP &amp; prioritisation</h2>
      <h3>MVP scope</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Automatic session tracking during recommendation and autoplay</li>
        <li>Save All and Select Songs flows</li>
        <li>Duplicate removal</li>
        <li>Basic AI ordering, sequencing only, not full conversational curation</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Conversational AI curation, social sharing and voice creation are deferred. They add cost and complexity without being required to prove the core hypothesis that one-tap saving increases conversion.</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Impact</th><th>Effort</th><th>Priority</th></tr></thead>

          <tr><td>Duplicate removal</td><td>Medium</td><td>Low</td><td><span class="pri pri--mvp">MVP</span></td></tr>
          <tr><td>Basic AI ordering</td><td>Medium</td><td>Medium</td><td><span class="pri pri--mvp">MVP</span></td></tr>
          <tr><td>AI conversational curation</td><td>High</td><td>High</td><td><span class="pri pri--p2">Phase 2</span></td></tr>
          <tr><td>Social sharing</td><td>Medium</td><td>Medium</td><td><span class="pri pri--p3">Phase 3+</span></td></tr>
          <tr><td>Voice playlist creation</td><td>Low</td><td>High</td><td><span class="pri pri--later">Not planned</span></td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>
    </section>

    <section id="metrics">
      <h2><span class="num">07</span>Metrics</h2>
      <div class="nstar">
        <span>North Star</span>
        <strong>Recommendation-to-Playlist Conversion Rate</strong>
        <p>The share of discovery sessions that result in a saved playlist. It measures the exact gap the feature exists to close, and it can't be gamed by playing more music.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Discovery</h6><ul><li>Songs played</li><li>Completion rate</li><li>Skip rate</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Activation</h6><ul><li>Discovery sessions created</li><li>Save-Session taps</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Conversion</h6><ul><li>Session to playlist rate</li><li>Songs saved per session</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Engagement</h6><ul><li>Playlist replays</li><li>Playlist completion rate</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Retention</h6><ul><li>D7 / D30 retention for users who save a session</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>AI quality</h6><ul><li>Playlist acceptance</li><li>Edit rate</li><li>Regeneration rate</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Guardrails</h6><ul><li>Playlist deletion rate</li><li>Recommendation dissatisfaction</li></ul></div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="experiments">
      <h2><span class="num">08</span>Experimentation</h2>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Experiment</th><th>Control</th><th>Treatment</th><th>Primary metric</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Save Session existence</td><td>No save-session entry point</td><td>Save Session button</td><td>Session to playlist conversion</td></tr>

      <p><strong>Hypothesis:</strong> one-tap saving and AI curation each independently increase the share of sessions converted into playlists, to be confirmed via the experiments above rather than assumed.</p>

    <section id="risks">
      <h2><span class="num">09</span>Trade-offs &amp; risks</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Automation vs. control</strong>: AI curation must stay editable; users should never lose the ability to override it.</li>
        <li><strong>Personalisation vs. discovery</strong>: over-personalising saved playlists risks narrowing future recommendations into an echo chamber.</li>
        <li><strong>AI quality vs. latency and cost</strong>: richer curation (LLM reasoning over audio features) costs more per playlist and adds latency; the MVP intentionally uses lightweight ordering.</li>
        <li><strong>Session scope</strong>: how much of a long session to offer for saving (10 songs vs. the entire session) affects both usefulness and clutter.</li>
        <li><strong>Groundedness risk</strong>: AI curation must only ever select from real catalogue and session tracks. This is enforced architecturally, not just by prompting.</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

    <section id="roadmap">
      <h2><span class="num">10</span>Roadmap</h2>
      <div class="road">
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 1 · MVP</b><p>Session tracking, Save All / Select, duplicate removal, basic ordering.</p></div>
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 2</b><p>AI curation from natural-language intent ("make this chill").</p></div>
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 3</b><p>Conversational playlist editing.</p></div>
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 4</b><p>Proactive AI music companion, for example "build me a 60-minute playlist for tomorrow's drive," informed by history, mood and context.</p></div>
      </div>
    </section>

      <h2><span class="num">11</span>Business impact</h2>
      <p>Where the value would show up, to be quantified post-launch:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Higher playlist creation and songs saved per active user</li>
        <li>Increased repeat listening and session length via revisited playlists</li>
        <li>Higher switching cost as personalised libraries grow</li>
        <li>Indirect contribution to retention and premium conversion through stronger perceived personalisation</li>
