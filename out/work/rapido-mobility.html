01India's last-mile mobility gap

India's urban mobility problem sits between two extremes: auto-rickshaws and cabs are relatively expensive and often unavailable on demand, while public transit rarely covers the final 3-10 km between a transit stop and a destination. Meanwhile, the country's enormous two-wheeler fleet sits idle for most of the day.

Rider problems
  • Costly short rides
  • Long waits
  • Unpredictable ETAs
  • Poor last-mile connectivity
Captain problems
  • Idle vehicle time
  • Unpredictable income
  • Fuel cost
  • Low customer reach
Platform problems
  • Balancing supply and demand
  • Low fares vs. attractive earnings
  • Positive unit economics
Core thesis

Two-wheelers are structurally well-suited to Indian traffic. They navigate congestion, need no parking search and are cheap to operate, making bike taxis a natural fit for short urban trips that are currently overserved by expensive cabs and underserved by unreliable autos.

Rider journey, where the friction sits

Open app→ Destination→ Price→ Captain search→ Accept→ Arrival→ Ride→ Pay / rate
  • Captain search: the highest-anxiety step; the uncertainty is whether a captain will be found at all.
  • Captain arrives: ETA reliability drives trust more than ETA speed alone.
  • Payment / rating: friction here suppresses repeat usage more than it appears to.

Captain journey, the underused moment

Go online→ Request→ Accept→ Travel to pickup→ Complete→ Payment→ Choose next location

"Choose next location" is the moment nobody instruments. Guidance here directly affects idle time and next-ride probability, which is why supply positioning made the AI MVP.

04Two-sided marketplace & liquidity

More captains→ Better availability→ Lower wait→ Better experience→ More riders→ Higher earnings↺

Liquidity: the platform's ability to reliably match supply and demand in real time, is the foundation everything else depends on. It's driven by supply density, demand density, match rate and time-to-match, and it flows directly into wait time, completion rate, satisfaction and repeat usage.

Liquidity→ Match rate→ Waiting time→ Ride completion→ Satisfaction→ Repeat usage
MVP principle

The first version should optimise for liquidity and completed rides, reliable matching, tracking and payment, rather than breadth of features. A polished app with unreliable matching fails; a bare-bones app with high liquidity succeeds.

06Product evolution & regulatory reality

Bike taxi→ Bike + auto→ Cabs→ Delivery / logistics→ Multimodal platform

Expanding beyond bikes serves several goals at once: it raises captain utilisation (a captain idle for bike rides may accept auto, cab or delivery jobs), diversifies revenue, deepens retention through one app for more trip types, and reduces exposure to any single vehicle category's regulatory risk.

Regulatory reality

Bike taxi legality in India varies by state and has been contested in several jurisdictions, commercial vs. private vehicle permit rules, licensing and insurance requirements differ by state and have changed over time. This is treated here as a live, evolving constraint requiring current, city-specific verification, not a settled fact assumed for the rest of the analysis.

It affects product strategy directly: which cities can launch bike taxis at all, how supply must be onboarded (permits, insurance, helmets), and how much unit economics can lean on this vehicle category long-term, reinforcing the strategic case for multimodal diversification.

Key insight

Captain earnings per hour matter more than simply adding more captains. Too much supply relative to demand drives down individual utilisation and earnings, which increases churn even as raw fleet size grows, a growth metric that quietly destroys the thing it's measuring.

09The AI opportunity

AI's role is to make the marketplace more liquid and more efficient at scale, forecasting demand, matching better than proximity alone, predicting problems before they happen, and personalising incentives instead of spending broadly.

AI featureWhat it doesBest technique
Demand forecastingPredicts demand by location/time from historical plus weather, event and traffic signalsTraditional ML (time-series / regression)
Intelligent matchingMatches on completion probability, not just proximity, the closest captain isn't always the best oneML + optimisation
ETA predictionBike-specific ETA from GPS, traffic and road typeTraditional ML
Cancellation predictionFlags high-risk assignments for proactive reassignment or incentiveTraditional ML (classification)
Captain AI CopilotSuggests repositioning toward predicted demandML forecast + rules; avoids unsafe / overwork nudges
Personalised incentivesTargets amount and timing per captain by need and churn riskML + optimisation
Safety intelligenceFlags route deviation and GPS anomalies for human reviewML anomaly detection + human escalation
Fraud detectionFlags GPS spoofing, fake rides, incentive abuseML risk scoring
Customer supportAuto-resolves low-risk issues, escalates high-risk onesLLM classification + human escalation
ML vs. LLM

Forecasting, matching, ETA, cancellation and fraud are numerical and structured problems best served by traditional ML and optimisation. LLMs add value where language understanding matters, support, the captain assistant, natural-language interfaces, not as a universal replacement for prediction models.

Evaluation runs offline against historical backtests before any online A/B rollout. Production monitoring tracks model drift, latency, feature drift and, critically, business impact, not just model accuracy in isolation.

Safety guardrails

High-risk actions, fraud flags, safety alerts, pricing changes, route through human review rather than fully automated decisions. Riders and captains can appeal automated decisions. Predictions and interventions are logged for audit and fairness review.

13Roadmap & competitive moat

Phase 1 · 0-3 months

Data infrastructure, demand forecasting, ETA intelligence.

Phase 2 · 3-9 months

AI matching, cancellation prediction, supply positioning, incentive optimisation.

Phase 3 · 9-18 months

Captain AI Copilot; early AI Mobility Agent recommending mode, price and ETA.

The moat

The durable advantage isn't any single model. It's the compounding loop of proprietary ride data, real-time marketplace signals and feedback tied to actual supply and distribution. A competitor could buy an LLM; replicating years of city-specific matching and demand data is far harder.

Future direction: an AI Mobility Agent that compares bike, auto and cab price and ETA against a stated goal, "I need to reach Sector 17 by 9 AM, affordably", and books on confirmation. That requires careful design around payment authorisation, safety checks and graceful failure handling before any autonomous booking is allowed.

14Final recommendation

  • Build an AI Marketplace Optimizer (demand forecasting → ETA → cancellation prediction → supply positioning) before broader AI features.
  • Why: these four levers most directly move the North Star and don't require matching or personalisation infrastructure to already exist.
  • For: riders (lower wait, more reliable ETA) and captains (better utilisation and earnings per hour).
  • Measured by: Completed Rides per Active Supply Hour, with wait time, cancellation rate and captain earnings/hour as supporting guardrails.
  • Biggest risks: regulatory uncertainty on bike taxis city-by-city, and incentive spend growing faster than genuine retention gains.
