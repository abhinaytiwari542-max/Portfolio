Case study 01 · Retention & incentive economics

Beyond discounts: an AI retention engine for Zomato

Food delivery is a multi-homing market: customers don't have to choose Zomato or Swiggy, they can use both. The easiest way to win one order is a discount. The easiest way to wreck unit economics is to make discounts the only reason customers come back.

01The problem

Zomato competes in a multi-homing marketplace: most customers have both Zomato and Swiggy installed, so switching cost is near zero and every order is a fresh competitive decision. The instinctive lever is discounting, but discounts are easy to match, expensive to sustain, and can train customers to only order when a promotion is active.

The real question is not how to give bigger discounts. It's how to retain customers efficiently: without discount spend growing faster than retention does.

Problem statement

Zomato needs to retain price-sensitive customers in a multi-homing marketplace without continuously increasing discount spend.

Working hypothesis

Giving the right customer the right incentive at the right time may retain more customers per rupee spent than giving everyone the same discount.

03Marketplace dynamics & competitive landscape

Zomato and Swiggy both operate two-sided marketplaces (restaurants + customers), and neither platform has an inherent lock-in. No claim is made here that either has better discounts, service or restaurant selection, those are open questions for research, and likely vary by city and segment.

The multi-homing flywheel

More restaurants→ Better selection→ More customers→ More orders→ More restaurant demand↺

Discounts can accelerate this flywheel in the short term, more orders means more restaurant interest. But if customers only respond to price, the flywheel becomes dependent on ever-increasing subsidy rather than genuine product loyalty.

Dimensions to research (not assumed)

  • Acquisition approach and promotional intensity
  • Loyalty / membership programme design
  • Delivery reliability and speed
  • Customer support responsiveness and issue resolution
  • Personalisation of offers and recommendations

04Retention funnel & the key insight

New user→ First order→ Second order→ Repeat→ Habitual→ Loyal

The first → second order transition is typically the highest-leverage drop-off point. It's the first evidence of habit formation, and the point where a generic new-user discount stops being the reason to return.

Key insight

The goal isn't to maximise discounts. It's to maximise incremental retention per rupee of incentive spend. A customer who would have reordered anyway represents wasted discount spend; a customer who reorders only because of the incentive represents real ROI.

Incremental Retention ROI = Incremental retained customers / Incremental discount spend
Cost per Retained Customer = Total incremental incentive cost / Incrementally retained customers

05The solution: Zomato Smart Rewards

An AI-powered retention engine that decides who should receive an incentive, when, what kind, how much to spend, and why: rather than applying one discount rule to everyone.

Decision flow

Customer data→ Feature engineering→ Churn model→ Offer propensity→ Expected incremental order→ Budget-constrained optimiser→ Best incentive

Which AI technique for which job

LayerUsed forWhy
Traditional MLChurn prediction, segmentation, incentive-response predictionStructured, high-volume, well-suited to classification/regression
OptimisationIncentive selection, budget allocation, experiment allocationConstrained decision-making under a fixed budget
LLMSupport-ticket summarisation, complaint classification, personalised offer copyBest for language understanding and generation, not numeric prediction
Architecture decision

An LLM is not used to predict churn or set discount amounts. That's a job for calibrated ML models where precision and auditability matter, and where you need to explain to a finance team why a specific rupee amount was chosen.

06Uplift modelling, the central idea

A high churn-probability score alone doesn't justify an incentive: some high-risk customers would churn regardless, and some low-risk customers wouldn't respond to an offer either. What matters is the incremental effect of the intervention.

Uplift = P(retention | incentive) − P(retention | no incentive)
CustomerRetention w/o incentiveRetention w/ ₹100UpliftGood target?
A40%60%+20 ptsYes, incentive changes behaviour
B85%87%+2 ptsNo, would return anyway
Why this matters

Customer A is the better incentive target even though Customer B has a far higher absolute retention probability. That gap is the difference between propensity modelling and uplift modelling: and it's the mechanism that keeps incentive spend efficient rather than merely well-targeted.

07Service recovery as a retention lever

Customer service is treated as part of the retention system, not a separate function. When a customer experiences a late delivery, missing item or quality issue, the system recommends an appropriate recovery action, refund, credit, free delivery, or priority support, with human policy as the final authority for sensitive or high-value cases.

SituationGeneric approachPersonalised approach
At-risk, price-insensitive customer"₹100 OFF" banner"Your next delivery is on us", matches known delivery-fee sensitivity
Churned 30+ daysNo message"We've missed you, ₹100 off your next order"
Loyal, frequent customerAnother discount"You've ordered 10 times this month, free delivery this weekend"
Recent late deliveryNothing / generic apology"Sorry your last order was late, free delivery next time"

08Product requirements

Goals
  • Increase incremental retention per rupee of incentive spend
  • Replace blanket discounting with targeted, explainable interventions
  • Treat service recovery as a first-class retention lever
Non-goals (v1)
  • Fully autonomous incentive agent with no human oversight
  • Dynamic real-time pricing
  • Restaurant-side supply optimisation

Representative user stories

  • As an at-risk customer, I want an offer relevant to why I might leave, not a generic discount.
  • As a loyal customer, I don't want to be given increasingly large discounts I don't need.
  • As a customer who had a bad delivery, I want proactive recovery, not silence.
  • As a product team, we want to measure incremental impact, not just coupon redemption.
  • As a product team, we want every AI-recommended incentive to have a clear, auditable reason.

Key non-functional requirements

Latency
  • Offer decision available before app-open render, <500ms from cache/precompute
Explainability
  • Every recommended incentive carries a human-readable reason code
Privacy
  • Order history, location and support data used only for retention modelling, never shared externally
Scalability
  • Churn and propensity scoring runs across the full active user base daily

Why a holdout group matters

Some customers would reorder with no incentive at all. Without a permanent control/holdout group, Zomato risks attributing organic reorders to discounts and systematically overstating incentive ROI, the failure mode that makes discount budgets grow forever while looking successful in every dashboard.

12Ethics, fairness & trade-offs

  • Price discrimination: different customers seeing different offers must be explainable and non-exploitative, not arbitrary.
  • Vulnerable customers: avoid incentive designs that exploit compulsive ordering behaviour.
  • Transparency: personalisation should be understandable to the customer, not a black box.
  • Privacy: order history, location and support interactions require strict access controls.
  • Notification fatigue: more targeting doesn't mean more messages. Over-targeting is a guardrail metric, not a success metric.
  • Model risk: a wrong churn or uplift prediction should default to a low-cost, low-risk action, not an aggressive discount.

13Roadmap

Phase 1

Rule-based segmentation, churn model, basic offers, A/B + holdout infrastructure.

Phase 2

Service recovery engine; expand the offer catalogue.

Phase 3

Uplift modelling for incentive targeting.

Phase 4

Dynamic, budget-constrained incentive optimisation.

Phase 5

Unified AI retention agent, operating within budget, fairness and escalation rules.

14Business impact & unit economics

Contribution Value = CLV − Acquisition Cost − Discount Cost − Delivery Subsidy − Support Cost

The framework's goal is to move discount spend from a blanket cost line to a targeted investment: reducing spend on customers who'd return anyway, and concentrating it on customers whose behaviour a well-chosen incentive can actually change. No revenue or margin figures are asserted here. They would be established through the experiments above.

15Summary

One line

Designed an AI-powered retention engine for Zomato that replaces blanket discounting with uplift-modelled, budget-constrained incentives and proactive service recovery.

The 50-word version

In a multi-homing food-delivery market, discounts alone don't build loyalty. I designed an AI retention system that predicts churn, models the incremental effect (uplift) of an incentive per customer, and selects the lowest-cost intervention (including service recovery) likely to actually change behaviour, validated through holdout-controlled experiments.

What this demonstrates

  • Reframed a discounting problem as an incremental-retention-ROI problem, then designed an uplift-modelling approach to target incentives at customers whose behaviour would actually change.
  • Designed an AI system architecture separating ML (prediction), optimisation (budget-constrained decisioning) and LLM (language tasks) by capability, rather than defaulting to one technique.
  • Built a full experimentation framework, including a permanent holdout group, to isolate incremental incentive impact from organic reorder behaviour.
