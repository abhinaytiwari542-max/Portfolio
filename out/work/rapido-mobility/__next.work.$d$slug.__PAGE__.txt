      <h2><span class="num">01</span>India's last-mile mobility gap</h2>
      <p>India's urban mobility problem sits between two extremes: auto-rickshaws and cabs are relatively expensive and often unavailable on demand, while public transit rarely covers the final 3-10 km between a transit stop and a destination. Meanwhile, the country's enormous two-wheeler fleet sits idle for most of the day.</p>

      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Rider problems</h6><ul><li>Costly short rides</li><li>Long waits</li><li>Unpredictable ETAs</li><li>Poor last-mile connectivity</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Captain problems</h6><ul><li>Idle vehicle time</li><li>Unpredictable income</li><li>Fuel cost</li><li>Low customer reach</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Platform problems</h6><ul><li>Balancing supply and demand</li><li>Low fares vs. attractive earnings</li><li>Positive unit economics</li></ul></div>
      </div>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Core thesis</span>
        <p>Two-wheelers are structurally well-suited to Indian traffic. They navigate congestion, need no parking search and are cheap to operate, making bike taxis a natural fit for short urban trips that are currently overserved by expensive cabs and underserved by unreliable autos.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="why">
      <h2><span class="num">02</span>Why bike taxis</h2>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Factor</th><th>Bike taxi</th><th>Auto</th><th>Cab</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Cost (3-10 km)</td><td>Lowest</td><td>Medium</td><td>Highest</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Traffic navigation</td><td>Best, lane-splits, avoids jams</td><td>Moderate</td><td>Weakest in congestion</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Parking / pickup friction</td><td>Minimal</td><td>Moderate</td><td>High in dense areas</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Capacity</td><td>1 rider</td><td>2-3 riders</td><td>3-4 riders</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Best-fit segment</td><td>Solo, price- and time-sensitive</td><td>Small groups, moderate budget</td><td>Comfort-seeking, longer trips</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>
      <p>The strategic sweet spot is the <strong>3-10 km urban trip</strong>: too short to justify a cab's cost and wait, too far or too congested for an auto to feel efficient, and exactly the range where a bike taxi's speed-through-traffic advantage compounds.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="users">
      <h2><span class="num">03</span>Users &amp; journeys</h2>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Persona</th><th>Profile</th><th>Core need</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Daily Commuter</td><td>Urban professional or student; cost- and time-sensitive; repeat weekday user</td><td>Fast, predictable, cheap commute</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Captain</td><td>Owns a two-wheeler; full- or part-time income</td><td>Predictable earnings <em>per hour</em>, not just per ride</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Occasional Rider</td><td>Low frequency, price-sensitive, situational</td><td>Availability when other options fail</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Ops Manager</td><td>Manages a city or zone</td><td>Tools to see and act on liquidity gaps in real time</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <h3>Rider journey, where the friction sits</h3>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">Open app</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Destination</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Price</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Captain search</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Accept</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Arrival</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Ride</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Pay / rate</span>
      </div>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Captain search</strong>: the highest-anxiety step; the uncertainty is whether a captain will be found <em>at all</em>.</li>
        <li><strong>Captain arrives</strong>: ETA <em>reliability</em> drives trust more than ETA speed alone.</li>
        <li><strong>Payment / rating</strong>: friction here suppresses repeat usage more than it appears to.</li>
      </ul>

      <h3>Captain journey, the underused moment</h3>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">Go online</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Request</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Accept</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Travel to pickup</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Complete</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Payment</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Choose next location</span>
      </div>
      <p><strong>"Choose next location"</strong> is the moment nobody instruments. Guidance here directly affects idle time and next-ride probability, which is why supply positioning made the AI MVP.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="liquidity">
      <h2><span class="num">04</span>Two-sided marketplace &amp; liquidity</h2>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">More captains</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Better availability</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Lower wait</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Better experience</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">More riders</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Higher earnings</span><span class="flow__arw">↺</span>
      </div>
      <p><strong>Liquidity</strong>: the platform's ability to reliably match supply and demand in real time, is the foundation everything else depends on. It's driven by supply density, demand density, match rate and time-to-match, and it flows directly into wait time, completion rate, satisfaction and repeat usage.</p>

      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Liquidity</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Match rate</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Waiting time</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Ride completion</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Satisfaction</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Repeat usage</span>
      </div>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">MVP principle</span>
        <p>The first version should optimise for liquidity and completed rides, reliable matching, tracking and payment, rather than breadth of features. <strong>A polished app with unreliable matching fails; a bare-bones app with high liquidity succeeds.</strong></p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="metrics">
      <h2><span class="num">05</span>Core metrics</h2>
      <div class="nstar">
        <span>North Star</span>
        <strong>Completed Rides per Active Supply Hour</strong>
        <p>It captures both marketplace health (matching is working) and captain value (utilisation), rather than optimising rides or supply growth in isolation, where either can be inflated at the other's expense.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="mgrid">
        <div><h6>Rider</h6><ul><li>Search-to-book conversion</li><li>Wait time</li><li>Cancellation rate</li><li>Repeat rate, NPS</li></ul></div>

        <div><h6>Marketplace</h6><ul><li>Match rate</li><li>Fulfilment rate</li><li>Supply-demand ratio</li><li>ETA accuracy</li><li>Avg. pickup distance</li></ul></div>
        <div><h6>Business</h6><ul><li>GMV</li><li>Contribution margin</li><li>CAC, LTV</li><li>Incentive cost</li></ul></div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section id="evolution">
      <h2><span class="num">06</span>Product evolution &amp; regulatory reality</h2>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">Bike taxi</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Bike + auto</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Cabs</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Delivery / logistics</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Multimodal platform</span>
      </div>
      <p>Expanding beyond bikes serves several goals at once: it raises captain utilisation (a captain idle for bike rides may accept auto, cab or delivery jobs), diversifies revenue, deepens retention through one app for more trip types, and reduces exposure to any single vehicle category's regulatory risk.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--risk">
        <span class="callout__label">Regulatory reality</span>
        <p>Bike taxi legality in India varies by state and has been contested in several jurisdictions, commercial vs. private vehicle permit rules, licensing and insurance requirements differ by state and have changed over time. This is treated here as a <strong>live, evolving constraint requiring current, city-specific verification</strong>, not a settled fact assumed for the rest of the analysis.</p>
        <p>It affects product strategy directly: which cities can launch bike taxis at all, how supply must be onboarded (permits, insurance, helmets), and how much unit economics can lean on this vehicle category long-term, reinforcing the strategic case for multimodal diversification.</p>
      </div>

    <section id="ai">
      <h2><span class="num">09</span>The AI opportunity</h2>
      <p>AI's role is to make the marketplace more liquid and more efficient at scale, forecasting demand, matching better than proximity alone, predicting problems before they happen, and personalising incentives instead of spending broadly.</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>AI feature</th><th>What it does</th><th>Best technique</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Demand forecasting</td><td>Predicts demand by location/time from historical plus weather, event and traffic signals</td><td>Traditional ML (time-series / regression)</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Intelligent matching</td><td>Matches on completion probability, not just proximity, the closest captain isn't always the best one</td><td>ML + optimisation</td></tr>
          <tr><td>ETA prediction</td><td>Bike-specific ETA from GPS, traffic and road type</td><td>Traditional ML</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Cancellation prediction</td><td>Flags high-risk assignments for proactive reassignment or incentive</td><td>Traditional ML (classification)</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Captain AI Copilot</td><td>Suggests repositioning toward predicted demand</td><td>ML forecast + rules; avoids unsafe / overwork nudges</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Personalised incentives</td><td>Targets amount and timing per captain by need and churn risk</td><td>ML + optimisation</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Safety intelligence</td><td>Flags route deviation and GPS anomalies for human review</td><td>ML anomaly detection + human escalation</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Fraud detection</td><td>Flags GPS spoofing, fake rides, incentive abuse</td><td>ML risk scoring</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Customer support</td><td>Auto-resolves low-risk issues, escalates high-risk ones</td><td>LLM classification + human escalation</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="callout callout--decision">
        <span class="callout__label">ML vs. LLM</span>
        <p>Forecasting, matching, ETA, cancellation and fraud are numerical and structured problems best served by traditional ML and optimisation. LLMs add value where language understanding matters, support, the captain assistant, natural-language interfaces, not as a universal replacement for prediction models.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

      <h2><span class="num">13</span>Roadmap &amp; competitive moat</h2>
      <div class="road">
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 1 · 0-3 months</b><p>Data infrastructure, demand forecasting, ETA intelligence.</p></div>
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 2 · 3-9 months</b><p>AI matching, cancellation prediction, supply positioning, incentive optimisation.</p></div>
        <div class="road__ph"><b>Phase 3 · 9-18 months</b><p>Captain AI Copilot; early AI Mobility Agent recommending mode, price and ETA.</p></div>
      </div>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">The moat</span>
        <p>The durable advantage isn't any single model. It's the compounding loop of proprietary ride data, real-time marketplace signals and feedback tied to actual supply and distribution. <strong>A competitor could buy an LLM; replicating years of city-specific matching and demand data is far harder.</strong></p>
      </div>

      <p><strong>Future direction:</strong> an AI Mobility Agent that compares bike, auto and cab price and ETA against a stated goal, <em>"I need to reach Sector 17 by 9 AM, affordably"</em>, and books on confirmation. That requires careful design around payment authorisation, safety checks and graceful failure handling before any autonomous booking is allowed.</p>

    <section id="recommendation">
      <h2><span class="num">14</span>Final recommendation</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Build</strong> an AI Marketplace Optimizer (demand forecasting → ETA → cancellation prediction → supply positioning) before broader AI features.</li>
        <li><strong>Why:</strong> these four levers most directly move the North Star and don't require matching or personalisation infrastructure to already exist.</li>
        <li><strong>For:</strong> riders (lower wait, more reliable ETA) and captains (better utilisation and earnings per hour).</li>
        <li><strong>Measured by:</strong> Completed Rides per Active Supply Hour, with wait time, cancellation rate and captain earnings/hour as supporting guardrails.</li>
        <li><strong>Biggest risks:</strong> regulatory uncertainty on bike taxis city-by-city, and incentive spend growing faster than genuine retention gains.</li>
      </ul>
