      <h2><span class="num">01</span>The problem: scale vs. personalisation</h2>
      <p>India's competitive-exam coaching market has a structural trade-off: high-touch coaching can offer individualised support but doesn't scale cheaply; affordable, mass-market platforms reach far more students but historically struggle to personalise at that volume.</p>
      <p>Physics Wallah built its position on affordability and reach. The open question this case study explores is whether AI can let a mass-market platform deliver some of the benefits of high-touch coaching, knowing what a student is weak at, what to study next, and when they need help, <strong>without a proportional increase in human cost</strong>.</p>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">Problem statement</span>
        <p>A student on a mass-market platform has access to hundreds of hours of content and thousands of questions, but often lacks personalised decision support: what to study today, what they're actually weak at, and whether they're on track.</p>
      </div>

    </section>

    <section id="persona">
      <h2><span class="num">03</span>Primary user: the at-risk JEE/NEET dropper</h2>
      <p>This persona is prioritised because droppers face the highest stakes with the least slack: they've already attempted the exam once, have known <em>and unknown</em> gaps, and are highly sensitive to falling behind schedule.</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Profile trait</th><th>Detail</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Prior attempt</td><td>Has already sat the exam at least once</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Knowledge state</td><td>Mix of solid topics and significant weak areas</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Behaviour</td><td>Inconsistent lecture-watching, inconsistent test performance</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Core pain</td><td>Doesn't know what to study next or whether they're on track</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="pquote">"I have hundreds of hours of content and thousands of questions, but I don't know what I should study today, what I'm actually weak at, or whether I'm on track for my target score."</div>
      <p>The problem is <strong>too much content with too little personalisation</strong>: not too little content. That distinction changes what you build.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="journey">
      <h2><span class="num">04</span>User journey: current vs. future</h2>
      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Current state</th><th>Future state, AI Learning Coach</th></tr></thead>

    <section id="solution">
      <h2><span class="num">05</span>The solution: AI Learning Coach</h2>
      <p>Positioned as a 24/7 personalised academic operating system, and explicitly <strong>not a replacement for teachers</strong>. It answers five questions for every student: What should I study? What am I weak at? What should I practise? Am I on track? When do I need human help?</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>What it does</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Diagnostic + Learning Profile</td><td>Topic-level mastery map from a diagnostic test plus performance history</td></tr>
          <tr><td>AI Study Plan</td><td>A specific daily plan (topic, duration, question count) that adapts to progress and fatigue</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Multimodal Doubt Solver</td><td>Student photographs a question; AI extracts, classifies and explains it</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Exam Readiness Score</td><td>Predicts topic-level risk and overall readiness, not just a raw percentage</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Slippage Detection</td><td>Flags when a student is falling behind syllabus pace, before it compounds</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Intervention Engine</td><td>Recommends a concrete recovery sequence, escalating to a teacher if needed</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Teacher Copilot</td><td>Surfaces at-risk students with an AI diagnosis so teachers focus where it matters</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>
    </section>

    <section id="hitl">
      <h2><span class="num">06</span>Human-in-the-loop design</h2>
      <p>The AI should not answer every doubt blindly. Confidence-tiered routing keeps humans in the loop for exactly the cases where AI is least reliable:</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Confidence</th><th>Action</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>High</td><td>AI answers directly</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Medium</td><td>AI answers, with a lightweight verification option</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Low</td><td>Routed to a human teacher</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table></div>

      <div class="callout callout--hyp">
        <span class="callout__label">On thresholds</span>
        <p>Any specific confidence cutoff (e.g. 95%) is illustrative only. Real thresholds require calibration against an expert-labelled evaluation set before launch, a number picked in a doc is a guess, not a threshold.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="callout callout--key">
        <span class="callout__label">The teacher's role in the future state</span>
        <p>AI absorbs first-line doubts, repetition and progress tracking, freeing teachers for complex problems, motivation, strategy and high-risk students, <strong>increasing teacher leverage rather than replacing them</strong>.</p>
      </div>
    </section>

      <h3>Why a knowledge graph + RAG, not a freeform LLM</h3>
      <div class="flow">
        <span class="flow__node">Subject</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Chapter</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Concept</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node flow__node--hot">Sub-concept</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Question</span><span class="flow__arw">→</span>
        <span class="flow__node">Common error</span>
      </div>
      <p>A knowledge graph lets the system locate <em>exactly</em> which sub-concept a student is weak in, Physics → Mechanics → Rotational Motion → Moment of Inertia, rather than reporting "you're weak at Physics." The doubt solver retrieves from approved course material, notes and question banks (RAG) rather than letting the LLM invent explanations, which matters when a wrong formula has real academic consequences.</p>

      <div class="tbl-wrap"><table>
        <thead><tr><th>Layer</th><th>Used for</th></tr></thead>

    <section id="risks">
      <h2><span class="num">08</span>Risks &amp; guardrails</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Hallucination</strong>: a wrong formula or explanation; mitigated by RAG grounding and confidence-based escalation.</li>
        <li><strong>Dependency</strong>: students may stop reasoning independently if answers are handed over too readily. The solver should default to explaining <em>concepts</em>, not just final answers.</li>
        <li><strong>Misdiagnosis</strong>: the system may mislabel a weakness; teacher review and student-reported corrections act as a check.</li>
        <li><strong>Bias</strong>: different student groups could receive uneven support quality; requires evaluation segmented by cohort, not just in aggregate.</li>
        <li><strong>Privacy</strong>: performance and doubt data are sensitive and require strict access controls and audit logging.</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
